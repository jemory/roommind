# Oriented solar input and room topology as declared model inputs

Per-room Extended Kalman Filters may take two further inputs, both supplied by the occupant rather than estimated: the compass azimuths of the room's exterior glazing, and the rooms it exchanges air with (`neighbour_rooms` through a doorway, `open_rooms` sharing an open-concept volume). With azimuths declared, the solar regressor becomes plane-of-array irradiance on the room's own facade — beam plus isotropic sky diffuse plus ground reflection, decomposed by the Erbs clearness-index correlation from the existing Kasten–Czeplak attenuation — instead of horizontal Global Horizontal Irradiance. With connections declared, the coupling regressor becomes a neighbour-weighted local reference temperature instead of the unweighted mean of every room. A room declaring neither keeps the previous horizontal-GHI, whole-house-mean pair, and its persisted model keeps learning undisturbed.

The solar change is forced by a measurable structural error rather than by preference. `max(0, cos(elevation)·cos(solar_azimuth − surface_azimuth))` is the *beam* projection factor for a vertical plane, and multiplying it by GHI omits the diffuse and reflected components entirely: at 41.9 °N it under-reads a winter south facade by 60 % (271 against 679 W/m² at December noon) and reports a north facade as exactly zero, where diffuse sky and ground reflection still deliver some 30 W/m². The more damaging error is in the time structure, which no fitted gain can absorb: a single scalar on a noon-peaked horizontal regressor cannot represent a west room whose gain peaks at 16:00, and the best such fit still leaves 30 % RMS and 71 % peak residual of that room's solar drive. Because `beta_s` is fitted, magnitude error is largely harmless in-sample; shape error is not.

This ADR amends [0001](0001-mean-field-house-thermal-coupling.md) without reversing it. Its rejection of an `N x N` pairwise conductance matrix stands, and for the reason it gives: individual inter-room conductances are not practically identifiable from closed-loop room air temperatures, because adjacent rooms track each other closely enough that the `(T_i − T_j)` regressor carries too little variance for the sensor noise. What that argument does not justify is the *unweighted global mean* as the reference. Two distinct things were conflated: the **support** of the coupling — which rooms interact, which is free, user-supplied, and not estimated at all — and the **magnitudes**, which are genuinely hard and stay with the filter. Supplying the support does not add unidentifiable parameters; it removes near-collinear ones, and it fixes two failures that a fitted scalar cannot compensate. The global mean lets a room exchange heat with rooms it does not touch, and it weighs a 3 m² closet exactly as much as a 40 m² living room. In a five-room simulation the best single gain for a kitchen open to three rooms came out *negative*; `alpha_house` is bounded at zero ("sealed off from the rest of the house"), so a well-connected kitchen converts to a sealed one — the wrong answer, not merely an imprecise one. Mean-field is in fact exact for two rooms, since `T_house − T_A = (T_B − T_A)/2` is a rescaled pairwise term, which is why the approximation looked sound while it was being written and degrades as the graph grows and becomes heterogeneous.

Two consequences are accepted. Real identities change when the inputs change, so each persisted model records the inputs it was fitted against (`regressor_signature`) and relearns only the affected parameters — `beta_s` for the solar input, `alpha_house` and `beta_fan` for the reference — on a signature mismatch; adopters therefore see a transient accuracy dip, and a house that configures nothing sees none. And an open-concept group is modelled as a single high-weight edge rather than merged into one thermal zone. Merging is what building-energy practice prescribes, and it remains the better model, but it would move room identity, entities, targets, schedules and occupancy for a change that the strong edge captures to first order; it is deferred rather than rejected.
